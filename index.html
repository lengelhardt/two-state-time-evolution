Two-State Time Evolution

two_state_time_evolution  |  v1.0  |  Sep 26, 2026

Phase, angular frequency and relative phase for a quantum system with two non-degenerate energy eigenstates.

This version assumes a time-independent Hamiltonian: H, its eigenstates and its eigenvalues stay fixed while the state evolves.

QUICK START

  1. Choose the initial state |ψ(0)⟩: one of the six spin states |±⟩z, |±⟩x, |±⟩y, a direction (θ, φ), or |c₂| and φ₂ directly.
  2. Choose the energies E₁ and E₂.
  3. Choose the measurement basis |±⟩: the plot shows P(+) and P(−), the probabilities of the two outcomes.
  4. Press ▶ PLAY. Watch each coefficient rotate in the complex plane at its own angular frequency.
  5. Compare with the Relative phase view: in a frame that rotates with c₁, only c₂ moves, and it moves at ω₂₁ = (E₂ − E₁)/ℏ.
  6. Use 1 / 2 / 4 VIEWS and each pane's menu to choose what is on screen.

THE PHYSICS

1. Stationary states and their phases

Assumption: the Hamiltonian is time independent. That is what makes the energy eigenstates stationary states and gives each coefficient the simple phase factor e−iEnt/ℏ. Changing E₁, E₂ or the eigenstates is not a time-dependent H: it defines a new H and restarts the clock at t = 0.

By default the energy eigenstates are |E₁⟩ = |+⟩z with energy E₁ and |E₂⟩ = |−⟩z with energy E₂ (a spin in a magnetic field along z). The small |±⟩z ✎ button in the ENERGIES panel changes them to |±⟩x, |±⟩y, or |±⟩n for any direction n̂ = (θ, φ). Writing |1⟩ = |E₁⟩ and |2⟩ = |E₂⟩, the Schrödinger equation gives

|ψ(t)⟩ = c₁(0) e−iE₁t/ℏ |1⟩ + c₂(0) e−iE₂t/ℏ |2⟩

Each coefficient keeps its magnitude and only its phase changes. Writing cn(t) = |cn| eiφn(t),

φn(t) = φn(0) − ωn t,    ωn = En/ℏ,    Tn = 2π/|ωn|

So each coefficient is a phasor: an arrow of fixed length |cn| turning in the complex plane at angular frequency ωn. Because of the minus sign, a positive energy turns the arrow clockwise. A negative energy turns it counter-clockwise.

2. The phase convention

An overall phase has no physical meaning, so it is chosen to make c₁(0) real and non-negative. The initial state is then set by just two numbers, both belonging to c₂:

c₂(0) = |c₂| eiφ₂,    c₁(0) = √(1 − |c₂|²)

In terms of the spin direction (θ from the z axis, φ around from the x axis) this is c₁ = cos(θ/2), c₂ = sin(θ/2) eiφ. The six buttons are the usual special cases:

|+⟩z = |1⟩|−⟩z = |2⟩
|+⟩x = (|1⟩ + |2⟩)/√2|−⟩x = (|1⟩ − |2⟩)/√2
|+⟩y = (|1⟩ + i|2⟩)/√2|−⟩y = (|1⟩ − i|2⟩)/√2

The convention fixes the phase only at t = 0. As time runs, c₁(t) turns away from the real axis like any other coefficient.

If the energy eigenstates are not |±⟩z, the initial state is still entered in the z basis with this convention, and the coefficients that evolve are cn(0) = ⟨En|ψ(0)⟩. These are shown in the equation above the views and are generally complex. |±⟩n are written with the same convention: |+⟩n = cos(θ/2)|+⟩z + sin(θ/2)eiφ|−⟩z and |−⟩n = sin(θ/2)|+⟩z − cos(θ/2)eiφ|−⟩z.

3. Probabilities in the energy basis do not change

|cn(t)|² = |cn(0)|² for all t. The arrows never change length, so the probability of measuring E₁ or E₂ is constant. The Time plots view shows Re and Im parts oscillating while |c₁|² and |c₂|² stay flat.

4. Global phase versus relative phase

Multiplying the whole state by one overall phase eiα changes nothing measurable. Factor out c₁'s phase:

|ψ(t)⟩ = eiφ₁(t) [ |c₁| |1⟩ + |c₂| eiΔφ(t) |2⟩ ],    Δφ(t) = Δφ(0) − ω₂₁ t
ω₂₁ = (E₂ − E₁)/ℏ,    T₂₁ = 2π/|ω₂₁|

Only the relative phase Δφ is physical, and it evolves at the difference frequency. The Relative phase view shows this in a frame that rotates with c₁: c₁ stands still and c₂ turns at ω₂₁. Try BOTH E + 1: both arrows speed up, but Δφ and every measurable quantity are unchanged. Adding a constant to the Hamiltonian is unobservable.

5. What does oscillate: measurement probabilities

Choose a measurement basis |±⟩ (|±⟩z, |±⟩x, |±⟩y or |±⟩n). The probabilities of the two outcomes are

P(+) = |⟨+|ψ(t)⟩|²,    P(−) = |⟨−|ψ(t)⟩|²,    P(+) + P(−) = 1

If the measurement basis is the energy eigenbasis, both are constant. For any other basis they contain an interference term that depends on Δφ, so they oscillate at ω₂₁ (the Bohr frequency), not at ω₁ or ω₂. For example, with energy eigenstates |±⟩z and measurement basis |±⟩x:

P(+x) = ½ + |c₁||c₂| cos Δφ(t)

If |ψ(0)⟩ is itself an energy eigenstate, no probability changes at all: a stationary state has no time dependence in any observable.

6. The Bloch sphere

With the convention above, cos(θ/2) = |c₁|, sin(θ/2) = |c₂|, and the azimuth is φ = Δφ. The Bloch vector is (⟨σx⟩, ⟨σy⟩, ⟨σz⟩) = (2|c₁||c₂| cos Δφ, 2|c₁||c₂| sin Δφ, |c₁|² − |c₂|²), with |+⟩z at the north pole. Time evolution is a precession about the energy axis (drawn in orange and blue, labeled E₁ and E₂; the z axis by default) at ω₂₁: the latitude (the probabilities |cn|²) is fixed and only the longitude (the relative phase) changes. The green and yellow dots mark |+⟩ and |−⟩ of the measurement basis. If the state vector makes an angle γ with the green dot, then P(+) = cos²(γ/2) and P(−) = sin²(γ/2).

Units

The UNITS toggle (in DISPLAY OPTIONS) chooses physical units (the default) or ℏ = 1. Physical units depend on the system: eV and fs for a spin and μeV and ns for a qubit. Conveniently, ℏ = 0.6582 in both (0.6582 eV·fs = 0.6582 μeV·ns), so a period is T = h/E = 4.136 fs ÷ E(eV), or 4.136 ns ÷ E(μeV). With the spin defaults E₁ = 1 eV and E₂ = 2.5 eV: T₁ = 4.14 fs, T₂ = 1.65 fs and T₂₁ = 2.76 fs (a 1.5 eV gap is a near-infrared photon, the scale of atomic transitions). ℏ = 1 is dimensionless: energies in a unit ε, angular frequencies in ε/ℏ and time in ℏ/ε, so ω equals E numerically. Switching units keeps the energy numbers and resets t to 0.

CONTROLS

  • Initial state: the six buttons choose |±⟩z, |±⟩x, |±⟩y. (θ, φ) opens sliders for a direction in space, in units of π. |c₂|, φ₂ opens sliders for the |−⟩z coefficient directly, and the |+⟩z coefficient follows from normalization. The two slider modes describe the same state, so switching between them keeps it.
  • Measurement basis: |±⟩z, |±⟩x, |±⟩y or |±⟩n(θ, φ). The Measurement probabilities view plots P(+) and P(−); its −/+ buttons zoom the time axis.
  • Resetting the clock: changing the initial state, the energy eigenstates or an energy sets t back to 0. Changing the measurement basis does not, because it only changes what is being measured.
  • Define Hamiltonian: the DEFINE HAMILTONIAN panel starts collapsed and shows a summary of the eigenstates and energies; click it to open it in place. EIGENSTATES opens a pop-up to choose |±⟩z (default), |±⟩x, |±⟩y or |±⟩n(θ, φ). MATRIX REPRESENTATION shows H as a 2×2 matrix in the z, x or y basis (or n, when the eigenstates are |±⟩n). The level diagram labels each line with its ket. Changing the eigenstates resets t to 0.
  • Energies: E₁, E₂ from −3 to 5 eV for a spin (−25 to 25 μeV for a qubit). The panel lists ω₁, ω₂, ω₂₁ and the matching periods. BOTH E ± 1 shifts the zero of energy.
  • Time: PLAY/PAUSE (or SPACE), reset to t = 0, step forward or back, and jump ahead by exactly one period T₁, T₂ or T₂₁. Every arrow that period belongs to returns to where it started.
  • Display: TRAILS show where each arrow was a moment ago. PHASE ARCS mark each phase angle from the real axis. ω ARROWS show the sense and rate of rotation, with the arc length proportional to ω. PROJECTIONS drop the Re/Im components onto the axes, and on the Bloch sphere drop the vector onto the equator.
  • Views: Rotating phasors, Relative phase, Bloch sphere, Measurement probabilities. Any pane can show any view.
  • Presentation tools: SET TO FULL SCREEN (top left), a laser pointer and a markerboard for drawing over the screen.

THINGS TO TRY

  1. Start in |+⟩z. The arrow turns, but does anything measurable change? Try every measurement basis.
  2. Start in |+⟩x and measure in the x basis. Pause and press + T₁. Which arrow returns to its starting position? Reset and press + T₂₁. What returns now, and what is P(+)?
  3. Start in |+⟩x and compare the x and y measurement bases. How are the two P(+) curves shifted relative to each other? Why?
  4. Set E₂ = 2E₁. How many turns does c₂ make while c₁ makes one?
  5. Press BOTH E + 1. Which views change and which do not? Why?
  6. Make E₁ negative. Which way does c₁ turn now?
  7. Slide E₂ toward E₁. What happens to T₂₁ and to the oscillation of P(+)?
  8. Start in |+⟩z, measure in the z basis, and change the energy eigenstates to |±⟩x. Why do P(+) and P(−) now swap back and forth between 1 and 0? Watch the Bloch sphere.
  9. Use (θ, φ) to start at θ = 0.25π and measure in the x basis. What is the largest P(+)? Check your answer on the Bloch sphere.
